# Pipeline

Prospects from first contact to signed client, plus the conversion math. Sourced from the client notes and the roster tables in [[../CLIENTS/00-Active-Clients]].

---

## Stages

Five stages. Each has an entry condition and an exit condition, so stage is a fact about what happened rather than a feeling about how it's going.

| Stage | Enters when | Exits when | Owner set? |
|-------|-------------|------------|------------|
| **Prospect** | Identified and audited by the Sales Outreach Agent; not yet contacted | First outbound email sends | Not required |
| **Pitched** | Outbound sent — email, audit, or demo delivered | They reply, or the follow-up sequence runs out | Yes |
| **Evaluating** | They replied and are considering; budget, timing, or internal approval outstanding | They give a decision, or go quiet past the stall threshold | Yes |
| **Hot** | Verbal intent, pricing discussed, only paperwork or a start date remaining | Signed, or lost | Yes |
| **Live** | Signed and the build has started | Moves off the pipeline into the client roster | Yes |

Two terminal states outside the ladder: **Lost** (with a reason — price, timing, went elsewhere, no response) and **Dormant** (no answer, worth re-approaching next quarter). Lost with no reason recorded is a lost lesson; make the reason required, the same way rejection reasons are required in [[02-Task-Queue]].

Stages are ordered but not one-way. A prospect can go from Evaluating back to Pitched after a re-pitch, and the transition history is what makes cycle-time measurable.

## Where the data lives

Today the roster lives as markdown tables in [[../CLIENTS/00-Active-Clients]] — Live Clients, Hot Prospects, Evaluating.

Parsing those tables is possible but brittle. A table is a *rendering* of the data, not the data: column order changes, someone adds a note in a cell, a row wraps, and the parser silently drops a prospect. And there are already two writers for the same fact — the roster table and the individual client note.

**Recommendation: stage lives in each client note's frontmatter, and the roster tables are generated from it.**

```yaml
stage: evaluating
stage_since: 2026-08-10
next_step: follow up on budget
owner: nick
plan_value: $X
industry: IV therapy
source: outreach agent
```

The dashboard reads frontmatter (structured, one field, one writer). The tables in `00-Active-Clients.md` get regenerated by the write-back step in Phase 4 of [[Dashboard-Build-Spec]], so they stay accurate for anyone reading the vault without the app. Until write-back exists, treat the tables as human-maintained and the frontmatter as authoritative for the dashboard — and reconcile the two in the diagnostics strip so drift is visible instead of quiet.

`stage_since` is the field that makes everything else on this page possible. Without it there is no aging, no cycle time, and no cohort.

## The view

**Board by stage** — count and list per column, ordered by days in stage descending so the stalest thing is at the top of each column. With ten to twenty prospects, the list *is* the report; nobody needs a funnel chart to look at fifteen rows.

**Per prospect:** name, industry, stage, days in stage, owner, next step, plan value $X, last contact, and a link to the audit the Sales Outreach Agent produced.

**Stall flags** — days in stage past a threshold:

| Stage | Stall after | Then |
|-------|-------------|------|
| Prospect | 7 days | Not yet contacted; either send or drop it |
| Pitched | 10 days | Follow-up due |
| Evaluating | 21 days | Direct ask, or move to Dormant |
| Hot | 14 days | Something is blocking; find out what |

A prospect sitting in Evaluating for six weeks with no next step is the pipeline's most common failure, and it looks identical to a healthy pipeline in a headline count. The aging column is what makes it visible.

## Conversion rate

Target per [[../BUSINESS/01-Financials]]: **10%** (prospects → clients). Actual: X% — placeholder until there is enough closed data to compute it.

The definition matters more than the number, because the obvious way to compute it is wrong:

> Naive: *(clients won this month) ÷ (prospects added this month)*.

That divides an outcome by an unrelated input. If the sales cycle runs six weeks and outreach volume changes month to month, the ratio swings wildly for reasons that have nothing to do with how well the pitch converts. In a month with heavy outreach and no closes yet, it reads near zero — right as the pipeline is filling up.

**Use cohorts instead.** Group prospects by the month they entered, and track what fraction of each cohort has reached Live. Report:

- **Cohort table** — entry month, cohort size, reached Live, still open, lost, conversion %. Cohorts younger than the typical cycle are marked *still maturing* and excluded from the headline.
- **Trailing rate** — closes ÷ prospects entered, over a trailing window at least twice the median cycle length (start with 12 weeks). One number, honestly caveated, for the headline tile.
- **Median cycle time** — days from Prospect to Live for won deals, computed from stage transitions. This is what tells you how long a cohort has to mature before its number means anything, so it is a prerequisite for the two above rather than a nice-to-have.

Also worth showing, since it separates two very different problems: **stage-to-stage rates** (Prospect → Pitched → Evaluating → Hot → Live). A weak Prospect → Pitched rate is a volume-and-follow-through problem; a weak Hot → Live rate is a pricing or closing problem. The blended 10% hides which one is happening.

## Pipeline value

Weighted pipeline value = Σ (plan value $X × stage probability).

Worth building, worth being honest about: with roughly ten prospects, weighted pipeline is mostly noise. One deal moving stages swings the total by tens of percent, and the stage probabilities are guesses until there is closed-won history to fit them to.

So: show **count per stage plus total unweighted value $X** as the primary figure, and treat weighted value as a secondary number that only becomes meaningful once there are enough closed deals to derive real stage probabilities. Until then, any probability weights are made up, and a made-up number in a dashboard tile hardens into a fact within a month.

## Feeding the pipeline

New prospects arrive from the Monday Sales Outreach Agent ([[../AUTOMATIONS/02-Monday-Sales-Outreach]]), which finds and audits them before anything is sent. Every prospect the agent creates should land as a client note with `stage: prospect` and a link to its audit, so the pipeline is populated by the automation rather than transcribed by hand afterward.
